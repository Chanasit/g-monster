# A dome, not a circle: 18 columns wide against 16 rows tall, because a slime is defined by
# resting wider than it is high. Widest span is columns 3-20, which leaves three clear columns on
# each side -- the horizontal transforms need that room, and a blob has no limbs to spend it on.
#
# The eyes are the only detail, and they are holes: 2x4 clear rectangles at rows 15-18, low on the
# face the way the reference draws them. Nothing else survives here. The reference's highlights are
# lighter purple on purple, and Theme is two-tone -- a highlight would have to be a hole, and a hole
# in the top of the dome reads as a puncture, not as gloss. The mouth is one row of the reference
# and would be a 1x1 cell here, which the 3x upscale turns into a speck.

# Move cycle for nonce: four frames instead of the derived two, so the legs can actually
# step rather than the body just rocking. `move` is the one movement state -- walking and running
# both play it -- so this is drawn as a walk, which is what it reads as at either cadence.
#
# Only the legs are drawn per frame. The body, head, ears and eye are the base grid above,
# byte-for-byte, translated as one block and never redrawn. That is deliberate. The reference art
# is 36x40 against this 24x24 grid, so it has to come down 2:1, and at that ratio it loses the
# creature: the silhouette shrinks from 20 columns to 18, and the pupil (drawn 4x4, 4x3, 3x4 and
# 4x3 across the four frames) lands on a different half-cell each time and flickers between 2x2
# and 1x1. Walking then showed a smaller Nonce with a twitching eye than the one standing
# still. Redraw the limbs onto this grid; never resample the whole creature.
#
# Frames are Blair's four keys: contact / passing-down / contact-mirrored / passing-up, folding
# his `down` and `up` extremes into the vertical offset of the two passing poses. The head sits on
# row 2, 3, 2, 1 across the cycle -- that is the dashed height curve in assets/blair-walk.jpg, and
# it is the only thing keeping the two passing poses distinct.
#
# The legs alternate spread (both feet planted, contact) and together (mid-stride, passing), and
# which foot is flat flips between the two contacts: frame 0 plants the left foot wide and lets the
# right toe off, frame 2 is that mirrored. A front-facing symmetric silhouette has no other way to
# say which foot is forward -- mirroring alone would produce the identical drawing.
#
# On the down frame the body drops a row but the feet do not: the legs shorten to rows 21-22, which
# is the knee bend absorbing the weight. On the up frame the whole creature lifts, feet included, so
# it clears the ground at the stride's high point.

# Sleep cycle for nonce: three frames, because the Z has to travel and two positions read as a
# flicker rather than as drift. At SLEEP_FRAME_MS the cycle is 4.5s.
#
# The derived sleep is the base grid squashed to 60% and dropped a row -- a standing creature
# compressed, with its eye still open. This one is drawn as a resting pose instead: the body sits
# lower and rounder on row 21, and the 2x2 pupil is replaced by a 4-wide lid line floating in the
# same clear face field. A closed eye is the whole reason this state is legible at 24x24; without
# it a sleeping Nonce is just a shorter Nonce.
#
# One Z at a time, rising and growing (3x3 at rows 5-7, 4x4 at rows 2-5, 5x5 at rows 0-4) and then
# gone. Three on screen at once is clutter at this size. The Z stays clear of the ears: at rows 8/9
# it would touch the left ear diagonally and read as an antenna.

# Idle cycle for nonce: four frames, so the eye can travel and come back.
#
# The pupil is the only 2x2 hole in the face field, and the field's clear interior is columns 9-14
# -- the cheeks close in at 8 and 15. A 2x2 pupil that keeps a clear column on both sides therefore
# has exactly three places to sit: 10-11, 11-12, 12-13. Left and right share no cell, so the two
# extremes are a full pupil-width apart, which is the most travel this face allows. Push it to 9-10
# and it welds onto the cheek and stops being an eye.
#
# Frames are left / centre / right / centre. The scan reverses instead of wrapping, because a
# right-to-left jump across the whole field reads as a flinch at 500ms a frame.
#
# The breath stays on its 1Hz derived cadence -- body up on the two centre frames -- but it lifts
# rather than drops, since an override may not put ink on row 23. The feet stay planted and the
# legs gain a row instead: the chest rises, which is what a breath looks like. The two centre
# frames are deliberately the same drawing; the eye passes through centre twice a cycle.

# Idle cycle for slime: four frames, because the derived bob is wrong for a blob. A creature with
# legs breathes by translating; a body of liquid breathes by changing shape, and shifting the whole
# dome down one row reads as the slime hopping, not as it settling.
#
# Frames are neutral / squash / neutral / stretch, so the cycle passes through rest twice and never
# jumps between the two extremes. At FRAME_MS that is a 2s breath and it loops seamlessly.
#
# Every frame carries exactly 240 ink cells, the same count as the base grid. That is the whole
# trick: a slime has a fixed volume, so what it loses in height it must gain in width. Squash is two
# rows shorter and two columns wider on each side; stretch is two rows taller and one column
# narrower. Draw either one without conserving the area and the creature appears to inflate.
#
# The eyes ride the deformation instead of staying put -- down a row and further apart in the
# squash, up a row and closer together in the stretch. Holding them still while the body around them
# moves is what makes a squashed sprite look like a resized sprite. They stay 2x4 in every frame:
# the hole is the only feature, and it has to survive at the same size throughout.
#
# The silhouette sits on row 22 in all four frames. The slime rests on the floor and does not leave
# it -- lifting the whole body is the idle of something with feet.

# Move cycle for slime: a hop, because Blair does not apply to a creature with no legs. The derived
# transform counter-shifts rows 17-22 against the body to fake a stride, and on a blob that is a
# skirt sliding out from under a torso -- there is no joint there for the eye to accept.
#
# Frames are crouch / launch / apex / land, which is the whole of a jump: gather, extend, float,
# splat. Anticipation is skippable on a two-frame lunge but not here; a hop that starts at full
# extension has nothing that reads as pushing off, and the creature looks dropped rather than
# launched.
#
# The hop is vertical. It carries no lean and no horizontal drift, and that is forced: `move` is the
# one action with no mirrored slot, and BattleView draws an approaching enemy with ACTION_MOVE and
# FACE_LEFT. A forward-leaning hop would therefore point the right way for the player's creature and
# the wrong way for every enemy sliding in. Height is the part of a jump that survives the missing
# direction -- no creature here translates across the screen anyway, nonce's walk included.
#
# Volume is conserved at 240 ink cells per frame, as in the idle. It does more work here: the crouch
# is 13 rows tall and 22 wide, the launch 20 rows tall and 12 wide, and squash-and-stretch is only
# convincing while the two trade off exactly. The eyes stay 2x4 throughout and ride the body -- low
# and wide apart in the crouch, high and close in the launch.
#
# Only the apex leaves the floor: the other three frames rest ink on row 22. A hop whose contact
# frames float reads as a creature bobbing in mid-air.

# Sleep cycle for slime: shut eyes and a rising stream of z's, because the derived sleep is a
# squash of the base grid and a squashed slime is indistinguishable from the crouch frame of its
# own hop. The state has to say "asleep" on a still frame, and on a creature with no posture the
# only things that can say it are the eyes and the symbol.
#
# The eyes are the same holes as everywhere else, turned on their side: 2 rows tall and 4 columns
# wide instead of 4 tall and 2 wide. Total hole area is unchanged at 8 cells per eye, which is what
# keeps the silhouette the same size as the idle -- a closed lid drawn as a 1-row slit would shrink
# the holes and quietly fatten the body. A lid must stay 2 rows: at 1500 ms a single row of clear
# reads as a rendering seam, not as an eyelid.
#
# The z's are 3x3, the smallest glyph where a diagonal still separates the two bars -- 2x2 is a
# blob and 4x4 does not fit three of them. They stack on rows 0-2, 4-6 and 8-10 with a blank row
# between each, and step one column left as they rise. The blank rows are load-bearing: adjacent
# ink at 3x merges into one shape, and three merged z's are a bar. The same reason keeps the lowest
# z two clear columns off the dome on row 10.
#
# Four frames: 1 z, 2 z, 3 z, then the lowest one gone. The stream fills, then the bottom of it
# dissipates while a new one starts below, so the loop restarts without every z popping at once.
# At SLEEP_FRAME_MS that is a 6 s cycle -- slow enough that the count is readable as a count.
#
# The body breathes on the even/odd frames underneath: neutral is 14 rows tall and 20 wide, the
# settle is 13 rows and 22 wide. Both carry exactly 240 ink cells, the same volume as the base grid
# and the idle -- what it loses in height it gains in width, or the slime appears to deflate as it
# falls asleep. The settle also drops the lids one row and spreads them a column further apart, so
# the eyes ride the deformation rather than sitting still in a body that moves around them.
#
# The sleeping pose is flatter and wider than the idle and its bottom row is full width: awake the
# slime holds a dome, asleep it relaxes into a puddle and the floor is where the volume goes. Every
# frame rests on row 22 -- nothing about sleep leaves the ground.

# Fight stance for slime: two frames, hand-authored, because the derived fight is the dome shoved
# two columns sideways and lifted a row -- on a silhouette with no limbs that is the idle blob
# sliding, and it carries no direction at all. `fight` is the one state that must face its target:
# ACTION_FIGHT_LEFT is this grid mirrored and it is what the enemy plays.
#
# The turn is three-quarter, not full profile. A profile is drawn by putting a limb edge-on and
# occluding the far one, and a blob has neither -- turning a featureless dome ninety degrees
# produces the same dome. What is left is mass and eyes, so both are used: the crest leans right
# over columns 12-16, the rear edge falls away to the left in an unbroken slope from column 12 at
# the crest to column 3 by row 20, and the leading edge bulges out to column 22 at rows 15-16. The
# eyes slide toward that leading edge with it -- far eye at columns 11-12, near eye at 17-18, both
# 2 wide and 4 tall as they are everywhere else on this creature. The turn is carried by the mass;
# the holes only have to agree with it, and resizing them to fake perspective would make the one
# feature on the silhouette a different size in every state for two columns' worth of depth.
#
# The stance is reared: 19 rows tall against the idle's 16, standing on a 14-column footprint where
# the idle spreads 18. A slime with no legs braces by standing up and putting its weight forward,
# and height is the only thing here that can say "gathered".

# the whole body a row is the derived bob this override exists to replace. The crest settles one row

# Attacks for slime: three states of their own -- rock, paper, scissors -- rather than frames of the
# fight slot. The stance above is what the slime holds all through an encounter; these play only
# while a blow is being thrown, and BattleView picks one by the attack the turn resolved.
#
# They are hand-drawn or they do not exist. No transform turns a silhouette into a hammer, so the
# generator emits these three bitmaps for a species only where sprites.txt draws them, and every
# other creature falls back to its stance. That is also the only way they fit: Rez.Drawables holds
# 254, the roster already spends 151, and three mirrored attacks across 30 species would want 180
# more. Six for one species costs 157 in total.
#
# The wheel is in the names, so nothing downstream has to remember which weapon beats which:
#
#   rock      CRUSH    the slime *is* the hammer -- whole body reshaped, cocked on frame 0 and
#                      pounded into the floor on frame 1. See the note under the grids
#   paper     ABILITY  a wave -- the body reared into a curl over its own water, the lip pitching
#                      forward on frame 1. See the note under the grids. Paper covers rock, so the
#                      slime's paper is the one attack that takes the enemy in rather than hitting it
#   scissors  ENERGY   a chakram -- a ring held up and back, thrown down-and-forward on frame 1
#
# Two frames each, and they are strike frames rather than a breath: unlike the stance, this art is
# only on screen while the sprite is travelling, so the pair has to say "swinging" from either phase
# the wall clock happens to catch it in. Paper and scissors move the weapon and nothing else that
# can be helped -- the chakram travels a column right and a row down, the wave's lip throws a column
# right and a row down -- because at 500ms a frame, anything else that moves reads as a glitch
# rather than as force.
#
# Paper and scissors carry exactly 240 ink cells, as the stance and the base grid do. The rule earns
# its keep there: a slime that grows a weapon pays for it out of its own body, which is why the mass
# under each is squatter than the stance's -- paper spends nearly all of it, and what is left of the
# blob is the six-row band of water the curl stands on. Draw a weapon as an addition and the
# creature inflates every time it attacks.
#
# Rock is the exception on both counts, and deliberately -- see the note above its grids.
#
# All three face right -- weapon out to the right, mass gathered back and left, eyes 2x4 as
# everywhere else on this creature. The generator mirrors each into rock_left / paper_left /
# scissors_left, so the enemy swings toward its target and not away.
#
# Rock: the slime does not pick a hammer up, it becomes one. There is no torso in either frame --
# the whole silhouette is head and handle, and the eyes punched into the head are the only thing
# left saying which creature this is. A blob holding a mallet was the earlier draft and it read as
# a brick on a stick, because at 24x24 the body ate the columns the head needed to be wide in.
#
# Frame 0 is cocked: the head lies across the top, rows 1-7, a 16-wide bar spanning columns 5-22,
# and the handle drops from under its middle in a 6-wide diagonal down-left to the floor at columns
# 4-9. Frame 1 is the impact, a rotation of about ninety degrees rather than a drop -- the handle
# butt stays on the floor at the left, and the head swings from the top of the canvas to a 9-wide
# slab standing on rows 8-22 at columns 14-22. That the slab reaches row 22 is the whole point: a
# pound that stops short of the floor is a wave.
#
# The handle joins the middle of the head in both frames, never an end. It was drawn joining an end
# first and the result was an elbow -- one continuous stroke bending, because a bar with the shaft
# under one tip is an arrow, not a hammer. The head has to overhang the shaft on both sides for the
# T to survive at 72px; frame 0 overhangs four columns to the left of the shaft and six to the
# right, frame 1 four rows above the joint and seven below it.
#
# The eyes are the only thing left saying which creature this is, so they stay 2x4 and stay in the
# head: columns 11-12 and 15-16 over rows 2-5 in frame 0, columns 16-17 and 19-20 over rows 12-15
# in frame 1 -- both pairs sit in the head's middle, where the slab is thick enough that they read
# as holes rather than as a bitten edge.
#
# Both frames carry 186 ink cells, equal to each other but well under the 240 every other slime
# state holds. Volume parity is what makes squash-and-stretch read as one creature, and it is worth
# breaking exactly here: 240 cells of hammer means a handle as thick as the head, and a bar joined
# to a bar of its own width is a bent tube. The head-to-handle contrast is what makes the shape a
# hammer at this size, so the mass goes and the silhouette stays.
#
# Paper: the slime does not throw water, it becomes the wave. The body rears into a curl over its
# own trough -- the back slope falls away to the left from column 7 at the crest to column 1 by row
# 16, the crest lies across rows 2-3, and the lip hooks down the right side and tucks back inward at
# the tip, columns 15-17 on frame 0's row 12. A hook that only descends is a wall of water; the
# inward tuck is the whole difference between a breaking wave and a slope.
#
# The barrel it encloses is by far the largest hole on this creature, and it stays open to the
# right: columns 21-23 are clear from the crest down to the water, so the mouth faces the target.
# Closing the ring was the first draft and it read as a doughnut -- one big hole ringed by ink is a
# shape with no direction in it, which is the one thing this state has to have.
#
# The eyes cannot ride the curl. The arc is four cells thick and a 2x4 hole punched through it eats
# the stroke, so they stay where the base grid puts them -- low, rows 18-21 at columns 5-6 and
# 11-12, punched through the band of water the wave stands on. That band is load-bearing for another
# reason: a curl drawn on its own is a shell, and six rows of flat water under it are what make it
# read as sea.
#
# Frame 1 is the pitch. The lip swings out a column and its tip drops a row -- to column 21 and row
# 13, from column 20 and row 12 -- and the crest's back edge slides a column right after it, so the
# barrel narrows from above while its mouth stays open. From row 5 down the back it is frame 0 byte
# for byte: slope, water, eyes. Both frames carry 240 cells, and the three the lip gains are the
# three the crest gives up.
