# Slime is the first species drawn at 72x72 instead of 24x24. It ends as the same bitmap either
# way -- the generator scales a 24 grid 3x and a 72 grid 1x, both onto 72px -- so the promotion
# costs no drawable, no resource and no code, only the grid. What it buys is everything a 24 grid
# cannot hold: at 3x a curve is a staircase of 3px steps, and any feature drawn one cell wide
# arrives as a 3px speck. Here a cell is a pixel.
#
# A dome, not a circle: 56 columns wide (8-63) against 48 rows tall (20-67), because a slime is
# defined by resting wider than it is high. It rests on row 67, and the last three rows stay blank
# -- the generator reserves the foot of a grid for the transforms that shift a body down, and at 72
# that reserve is three rows rather than one.
#
# Every feature is a hole. Theme is two-tone, so there is no third colour to shade with:
#
#   eyes    8 x 13, columns 21-28 and 43-50 over rows 41-53
#   gloss   a tilted crescent, columns 19-28 over rows 27-35, high on the left of the crest
#   mouth   a 3-thick curve, columns 26-45 over rows 57-63
#
# The gloss is the reason this grid is 72 and not 24. At 24 it would be about two cells, which the
# 3x upscale turns into a hard rectangle of nothing in the middle of the dome -- a puncture, not
# gloss. The mouth is the same argument: one row of the reference at 24, a speck at 3x, a legible
# curve here. Both were left out of the 24 grid on purpose, and they are what came back.
#
# 1837 ink cells. That is the volume every deformation of this creature holds to: a slime has a
# fixed volume, so what a pose loses in height it must gain in width, or the thing inflates and
# deflates as it moves. The three attacks are the documented exceptions -- see their notes.

# Move cycle for nonce: four frames instead of the derived two, so the legs can actually
# step rather than the body just rocking. `move` is the one movement state -- walking and running
# both play it -- so this is drawn as a walk, which is what it reads as at either cadence.
#
# Only the legs are drawn per frame. The body, head, ears and eye are the base grid above,
# byte-for-byte, translated as one block and never redrawn. That is deliberate. The reference art
# is 36x40 against this 24x24 grid, so it has to come down 2:1, and at that ratio it loses the
# creature: the silhouette shrinks from 20 columns to 18, and the pupil (drawn 4x4, 4x3, 3x4 and
# 4x3 across the four frames) lands on a different half-cell each time and flickers between 2x2
# and 1x1. Walking then showed a smaller Nonce with a twitching eye than the one standing
# still. Redraw the limbs onto this grid; never resample the whole creature.
#
# Frames are Blair's four keys: contact / passing-down / contact-mirrored / passing-up, folding
# his `down` and `up` extremes into the vertical offset of the two passing poses. The head sits on
# row 2, 3, 2, 1 across the cycle -- that is the dashed height curve in assets/blair-walk.jpg, and
# it is the only thing keeping the two passing poses distinct.
#
# The legs alternate spread (both feet planted, contact) and together (mid-stride, passing), and
# which foot is flat flips between the two contacts: frame 0 plants the left foot wide and lets the
# right toe off, frame 2 is that mirrored. A front-facing symmetric silhouette has no other way to
# say which foot is forward -- mirroring alone would produce the identical drawing.
#
# On the down frame the body drops a row but the feet do not: the legs shorten to rows 21-22, which
# is the knee bend absorbing the weight. On the up frame the whole creature lifts, feet included, so
# it clears the ground at the stride's high point.

# Sleep cycle for nonce: three frames, because the Z has to travel and two positions read as a
# flicker rather than as drift. At SLEEP_FRAME_MS the cycle is 4.5s.
#
# The derived sleep is the base grid squashed to 60% and dropped a row -- a standing creature
# compressed, with its eye still open. This one is drawn as a resting pose instead: the body sits
# lower and rounder on row 21, and the 2x2 pupil is replaced by a 4-wide lid line floating in the
# same clear face field. A closed eye is the whole reason this state is legible at 24x24; without
# it a sleeping Nonce is just a shorter Nonce.
#
# One Z at a time, rising and growing (3x3 at rows 5-7, 4x4 at rows 2-5, 5x5 at rows 0-4) and then
# gone. Three on screen at once is clutter at this size. The Z stays clear of the ears: at rows 8/9
# it would touch the left ear diagonally and read as an antenna.
#
# The body breathes neutral / down 1 / neutral. That is the only horizontal-free motion available,
# and sleep must stay vertical -- at 1500ms a frame, any sideways travel reads as a slide.

# Idle cycle for nonce: four frames, so the eye can travel and come back.
#
# The pupil is the only 2x2 hole in the face field, and the field's clear interior is columns 9-14
# -- the cheeks close in at 8 and 15. A 2x2 pupil that keeps a clear column on both sides therefore
# has exactly three places to sit: 10-11, 11-12, 12-13. Left and right share no cell, so the two
# extremes are a full pupil-width apart, which is the most travel this face allows. Push it to 9-10
# and it welds onto the cheek and stops being an eye.
#
# Frames are left / centre / right / centre. The scan reverses instead of wrapping, because a
# right-to-left jump across the whole field reads as a flinch at 500ms a frame.
#

# Idle cycle for slime: four frames, because the derived bob is wrong for a blob. A creature with
# legs breathes by translating; a body of liquid breathes by changing shape, and dropping the whole
# dome reads as the slime hopping, not as it settling.
#
# Frames are neutral / squash / neutral / stretch, so the cycle passes through rest twice and never
# jumps between the two extremes. At FRAME_MS that is a 2 s breath and it loops seamlessly.
#
# Volume holds across the cycle -- 1837, 1836, 1837, 1838 cells against the base grid's 1837. The
# squash is 64 wide and 42 tall, the stretch 50 wide and 53 tall: one slime, redistributed. Draw
# either without conserving the area and the creature appears to inflate.
#
# The eyes ride the deformation instead of staying put -- lower and further apart in the squash,
# higher and closer in the stretch. Holding them still while the body moves around them is what
# makes a squashed sprite look like a resized sprite.
#
# The gloss rides with them, but it is pinned above the eyes rather than to the crest, and that is
# not a preference: a highlight fixed to the top of the dome lands on the eye in the squash frame,
# and two holes that touch are one hole. A single hole that size reads as damage to the silhouette,
# not as a face.
#
# The silhouette sits on row 67 in all four frames. The slime rests on the floor and does not leave
# it -- lifting the whole body is the idle of something with feet.

# Move cycle for slime: a hop, because Blair does not apply to a creature with no legs. The derived
# transform counter-shifts the bottom rows against the body to fake a stride, and on a blob that is
# a skirt sliding out from under a torso -- there is no joint there for the eye to accept.
#
# Frames are crouch / launch / apex / land, which is the whole of a jump: gather, extend, float,
# splat. Anticipation is skippable on a two-frame lunge but not here; a hop that starts at full
# extension has nothing that reads as pushing off, and the creature looks dropped rather than
# launched.
#
# The hop is vertical. It carries no lean and no horizontal drift, and that is forced: `move` is the
# one action with no mirrored slot, and BattleView draws an approaching enemy with ACTION_MOVE and
# FACE_LEFT. A forward-leaning hop would point the right way for the player's creature and the wrong
# way for every enemy sliding in. Height is the part of a jump that survives the missing direction.
#
# Volume is conserved at 1830-1837 cells against the base grid's 1837. It does more work here than
# in the idle: the crouch is 42 rows tall and 64 wide, the launch 59 rows tall and 42 wide, and
# squash-and-stretch is only convincing while the two trade off exactly.
#
# The two contact frames are matched on height alone rather than by scaling the whole silhouette.
# They are already 64 and 66 columns wide against a 72-wide canvas, and a crouch that gains its
# volume sideways gets its flanks cut off square by the edge -- which reads as a rendering bug, not
# as weight.
#
# Only the apex leaves the floor: it sits on rows 8-56, and the other three rest ink on row 67. A
# hop whose contact frames float reads as a creature bobbing in mid-air.

# Sleep cycle for slime: shut eyes and a rising stream of z's, because the derived sleep is a squash
# of the base grid and a squashed slime is indistinguishable from the crouch frame of its own hop.
# The state has to say "asleep" on a still frame, and on a creature with no posture the only things
# that can say it are the eyes and the symbol.
#
# The lids are the promotion earning its keep. At 24 a shut eye was the open hole turned on its side
# -- 4 cells wide by 2 tall, because a 1-row slit at 3x is a rendering seam rather than an eyelid.
# Here they are curves: a 16 x 6 lash bowing down over each socket, which is a closed eye rather
# than a hole that happens to be lying down.
#

# within six of the base grid's 1837, the settle three rows shorter and six columns wider. The z's are
# ink on top of that and not part of the creature -- about 70 cells each, which is why the frame
# totals climb 1903, 1983, 2043, 1983 while the slime itself does not change size.
#
# The sleeping pose is flatter and wider than the idle: awake the slime holds a dome, asleep it
# relaxes into a puddle and the floor is where the volume goes. Every frame rests on row 67 --

# Fight stance for slime: two frames, hand-authored, because the derived fight is the dome shoved
# sideways and lifted -- on a silhouette with no limbs that is the idle blob sliding, and it carries
# no direction at all. `fight` is the one state that must face its target: ACTION_FIGHT_LEFT is this
# grid mirrored and it is what the enemy plays.
#
# The turn is carried by the whole mass, because a blob has nothing else to turn with. There is no
# far ear to occlude and no limb to put edge-on, and moving the eyes inside a symmetric dome reads
# as a lazy eye rather than as a head turn. So the silhouette shears: the crest is carried out over
# the leading edge, the rear falls away to the left in one unbroken slope, and the right flank
# swells at mid-height where the weight is gathered. The eyes follow it -- both forward of centre,
# columns 26-33 and 44-51 -- and the mouth sits under them on the same side.
#
# The stance is reared and narrow-footed: 59 rows tall against the idle's 48, in 49 columns against
# 56. A slime with no legs braces by standing up and putting its weight forward, and height is the
# only thing on this creature that can say "gathered".
#
# Frame 1 is the breath, and on a blob a breath is a change of shape rather than a translation --
# dropping the whole body a row is the derived bob this override exists to replace. The lean goes
# further over the leading edge and the flank rolls forward with it, so the silhouette reaches
# column 59 where frame 0 stops at 55, while the crest settles three rows.
#
# 1833 and 1831 cells against the base grid's 1837. Volume parity is what makes squash-and-stretch
# read as one creature deforming rather than two sprites of different sizes.
#
# Two frames, not four, because the frame index comes off the wall clock: the cycle's phase has
# nothing to do with when the blow lands, since BattleView throws the attack itself over LUNGE_MS.

# Attacks for slime: three states of their own -- rock, paper, scissors -- rather than frames of the
# fight slot. The stance above is what the slime holds all through an encounter; these play only
# while a blow is being thrown, and BattleView picks one by the attack the turn resolved.
#
# They are hand-drawn or they do not exist. No transform turns a silhouette into a hammer, so the
# generator emits these three bitmaps for a species only where sprites.txt draws them, and every
# other creature falls back to its stance. That is also the only way they fit: Rez.Drawables holds
# 254, the roster already spends 151, and three mirrored attacks across 30 species would want 180
# more. Six for one species costs 157 in total.
#
# The wheel is in the names, so nothing downstream has to remember which weapon beats which:
#
#   rock      CRUSH    the slime *is* the hammer -- whole body reshaped, cocked on frame 0 and
#                      pounded into the floor on frame 1. See the note under the grids
#   paper     ABILITY  a wave -- the body reared into a curl over its own water, the lip pitching
#                      forward on frame 1. See the note under the grids. Paper covers rock, so the
#                      slime's paper is the one attack that takes the enemy in rather than hitting it
#   scissors  ENERGY   a chakram -- a ring of goo held up and back, thrown down-and-forward on
#                      frame 1
#
# All three face right -- weapon out to the right, mass gathered back and left. The generator mirrors
# each into rock_left / paper_left / scissors_left, so the enemy swings toward its target and not
# away from it.
#
# Two frames each, and they are strike frames rather than a breath: this art is only on screen while
# the sprite is travelling, so the pair has to say "swinging" from either phase the wall clock
# happens to catch it in.
#
# None of the three is held to the 1837 cells the creature's own deformations conserve, and that is
# the point rather than an oversight. A hammer at 1837 cells is a handle as thick as its head, and a
# bar joined to a bar of its own width is a bent tube; a wave at 1837 cells is a curl with no sea
# under it, which is a shell. What each one conserves is its own volume across its two frames, which
# is what stops the weapon growing between them.
#
# Rock: the slime does not pick a hammer up, it becomes one. There is no torso in either frame --
# the whole silhouette is head and handle, and the eyes punched into the head are the only thing
# left saying which creature this is. A blob holding a mallet was the earlier draft and it read as a
# brick on a stick, because the body ate the columns the head needed to be wide in.
#
# Frame 0 is cocked: the head lies across the top over rows 3-28, spanning columns 13-67, and the
# handle drops from under its middle down to the left. Frame 1 is the impact, a rotation of about
# ninety degrees rather than a drop -- the handle butt stays down at the left and the head swings
# from the top of the canvas to a slab standing on rows 17-67 at columns 36-67. That the slab
# reaches the floor row is the whole point: a pound that stops short of it is a wave.
#
# The handle joins the middle of the head in both frames, never an end. It was drawn joining an end
# first and the result was an elbow -- one continuous stroke bending, because a bar with the shaft
# under one tip is an arrow, not a hammer. The head has to overhang the shaft on both sides for the
# T to survive at 72px.
#
# The face stays in the head and rotates with it: eyes side by side over rows 6-19 in frame 0, one
# above the other over rows 26-58 in frame 1, with the mouth between them on the leading side. Both
# sit where the slab is thick enough that they read as holes rather than as a bitten edge.
#
# 1761 and 1760 cells -- equal to each other, four percent under the 1837 the creature's own poses
# hold. The head-to-handle contrast is what makes the shape a hammer at this size, so the mass goes
# and the silhouette stays.
