# Slime is the one species drawn at 72x72 instead of 24x24. It ends as the same bitmap either way --
# the generator scales a 24 grid 3x and a 72 grid 1x, both onto 72px -- so the promotion costs no
# drawable, no resource and no code, only the grid. What it buys is everything a 24 grid cannot hold:
# at 3x a curve is a staircase of 3px steps, and any feature drawn one cell wide arrives as a 3px
# speck. Here a cell is a pixel.
#
# Drawn from assets/slime-reference.png, a 14x11 pixel-art slime, so the proportions are its: a dome
# squared off at the shoulders, 57 columns wide (7-63) against 46 rows tall (22-67), because a slime
# is defined by resting wider than it is high. The profile is a superellipse and not a circle -- |x/28|^2.6 +
# |y/45|^2.6 = 1, lower half only -- and that exponent is what stands the flanks up over the bottom
# third instead of tucking them under. It rests on row 67, and the last three rows stay blank: the
# generator reserves the foot of a grid for the transforms that shift a body down, and at 72 that
# reserve is three rows rather than one.
#
# Every feature is a hole. Theme is two-tone, so there is no third colour to shade with:
#
#   eyes    8 x 16 upright rectangles, columns 25-32 and 46-53 over rows 42-57, 128 cells each
#   gloss   a tilted crescent, 69 cells, columns 30-44 over rows 29-38, high on the right
#
# The pair sits four columns right of the body's centre line and the highlight is on the same side.
# Both of those come off the reference, which is lit from the upper right and facing that way; a
# centred pair under a centred highlight is a blanker creature than the one being copied.
#
# There is no mouth, because the reference has none. The gloss is therefore the whole of what this
# grid spends its extra resolution on, and it is enough on its own: at 24 the crescent would be about
# two cells, which the 3x upscale turns into a hard rectangle of nothing in the middle of the dome --
# a puncture, not gloss.
#
# 1899 ink cells inside a 2224-cell silhouette. That silhouette figure is the volume every
# deformation of this creature holds to: a slime has a fixed volume, so what a pose loses in height it
# must gain in width, or the thing inflates and deflates as it moves. Ink totals alone do not compare
# across states, because a closed eye removes less than an open one -- the sleep frames are 78 cells
# heavier for that reason alone. The three attacks are the documented exceptions, and say so.

# Move cycle for nonce: four frames instead of the derived two, so the legs can actually
# step rather than the body just rocking. `move` is the one movement state -- walking and running
# both play it -- so this is drawn as a walk, which is what it reads as at either cadence.
#
# Only the legs are drawn per frame. The body, head, ears and eye are the base grid above,
# byte-for-byte, translated as one block and never redrawn. That is deliberate. The reference art
# is 36x40 against this 24x24 grid, so it has to come down 2:1, and at that ratio it loses the
# creature: the silhouette shrinks from 20 columns to 18, and the pupil (drawn 4x4, 4x3, 3x4 and
# 4x3 across the four frames) lands on a different half-cell each time and flickers between 2x2
# and 1x1. Walking then showed a smaller Nonce with a twitching eye than the one standing
# still. Redraw the limbs onto this grid; never resample the whole creature.
#
# Frames are Blair's four keys: contact / passing-down / contact-mirrored / passing-up, folding
# his `down` and `up` extremes into the vertical offset of the two passing poses. The head sits on
# row 2, 3, 2, 1 across the cycle -- that is the dashed height curve in assets/blair-walk.jpg, and
# it is the only thing keeping the two passing poses distinct.
#
# The legs alternate spread (both feet planted, contact) and together (mid-stride, passing), and
# which foot is flat flips between the two contacts: frame 0 plants the left foot wide and lets the
# right toe off, frame 2 is that mirrored. A front-facing symmetric silhouette has no other way to
# say which foot is forward -- mirroring alone would produce the identical drawing.
#
# On the down frame the body drops a row but the feet do not: the legs shorten to rows 21-22, which
# is the knee bend absorbing the weight. On the up frame the whole creature lifts, feet included, so
# it clears the ground at the stride's high point.

# Sleep cycle for nonce: three frames, because the Z has to travel and two positions read as a
# flicker rather than as drift. At SLEEP_FRAME_MS the cycle is 4.5s.
#
# The derived sleep is the base grid squashed to 60% and dropped a row -- a standing creature
# compressed, with its eye still open. This one is drawn as a resting pose instead: the body sits
# lower and rounder on row 21, and the 2x2 pupil is replaced by a 4-wide lid line floating in the
# same clear face field. A closed eye is the whole reason this state is legible at 24x24; without
# it a sleeping Nonce is just a shorter Nonce.
#
# One Z at a time, rising and growing (3x3 at rows 5-7, 4x4 at rows 2-5, 5x5 at rows 0-4) and then
# gone. Three on screen at once is clutter at this size. The Z stays clear of the ears: at rows 8/9
# it would touch the left ear diagonally and read as an antenna.
#
# The body breathes neutral / down 1 / neutral. That is the only horizontal-free motion available,
# and sleep must stay vertical -- at 1500ms a frame, any sideways travel reads as a slide.

# Idle cycle for nonce: four frames, so the eye can travel and come back.
#
# The pupil is the only 2x2 hole in the face field, and the field's clear interior is columns 9-14
# -- the cheeks close in at 8 and 15. A 2x2 pupil that keeps a clear column on both sides therefore
# has exactly three places to sit: 10-11, 11-12, 12-13. Left and right share no cell, so the two
# extremes are a full pupil-width apart, which is the most travel this face allows. Push it to 9-10
# and it welds onto the cheek and stops being an eye.
#
# Frames are left / centre / right / centre. The scan reverses instead of wrapping, because a
# right-to-left jump across the whole field reads as a flinch at 500ms a frame.
#

# rather than drops, since an override may not put ink on row 23. The feet stay planted and the

# Idle cycle for slime: four frames, because the derived bob is wrong for a blob. A creature with legs
# breathes by translating; a body of liquid breathes by changing shape, and dropping the whole dome
# reads as the slime hopping rather than as it settling.
#
# Frames are neutral / squash / neutral / stretch, so the cycle passes through rest twice and never
# jumps between the two extremes. At FRAME_MS that is a 2 s breath and it loops seamlessly.
#
# Volume holds across the cycle: 2224, 2189, 2224 and 2254 cells of silhouette against the base
# grid's 2224. The squash is 63 wide (columns 4-66) by 41 tall, the stretch 51 wide by 52 tall -- one
# slime, redistributed. Draw either without conserving the area and the creature appears to inflate.
#
# The eyes ride the deformation instead of staying put: rows 45-58 and nine columns apart in the
# squash, rows 39-56 and twelve apart in the stretch. Holding them still while the body moves around
# them is what makes a squashed sprite look like a resized sprite.
#
# The gloss rides with them, but it is pinned above the eyes rather than to the crest, and it keeps
# its size while it does. Neither is a preference. A highlight fixed to the top of the dome lands on
# the eye in the squash frame, and two holes that touch are one hole -- which reads as damage to the
# silhouette, not as a face. A highlight scaled with the body grew a fifth between the neutral and
# squash frames, and a feature that changes area on its own is a glitch rather than a breath. So all
# four frames wear the same 69-cell crescent, carried by the socket.
#
# The silhouette sits on row 67 in all four frames. The slime rests on the floor and does not leave
# it -- lifting the whole body is the idle of something with feet.

# Move cycle for slime: a hop, because Blair does not apply to a creature with no legs. The derived
# transform counter-shifts the bottom rows against the body to fake a stride, and on a blob that is a
# skirt sliding out from under a torso -- there is no joint there for the eye to accept.
#
# Frames are crouch / launch / apex / land, which is the whole of a jump: gather, extend, float,
# splat. Anticipation is skippable on a two-frame lunge but not here; a hop that starts at full
# extension has nothing in it that reads as pushing off, and the creature looks dropped rather than
# launched.
#
# The hop is vertical. It carries no lean and no horizontal drift, and that is forced: `move` is the
# one action with no mirrored slot, and BattleView draws an approaching enemy with ACTION_MOVE and
# FACE_LEFT. A forward-leaning hop would point the right way for the player's creature and the wrong
# way for every enemy sliding in. Height is the part of a jump that survives the missing direction.
#
# Volume is conserved at 2189-2205 cells of silhouette against the base grid's 2224, and it does more
# work here than in the idle: the crouch is 41 rows tall and 63 wide, the launch 55 rows tall and 47
# wide. Squash-and-stretch is only convincing while the two trade off exactly.
#
# Only the apex leaves the floor: it sits on rows 10-58, nine rows clear, and the other three rest ink
# on row 67. A hop whose contact frames float reads as a creature bobbing in mid-air.
#
# The land frame is the widest pose the creature has at 65 columns, and its eyes come back in two
# columns for it -- at the mapped position the right socket keeps only 2px of ink between itself and
# the flank, and a hole that close to the edge reads as a bite out of the silhouette.

# Sleep cycle for slime: shut eyes and a rising stream of z's, because the derived sleep is a squash
# of the base grid and a squashed slime is indistinguishable from the crouch frame of its own hop. The
# state has to say "asleep" on a still frame, and on a creature with no posture the only things that
# can say it are the eyes and the symbol.
#
# The lids are the promotion earning its keep. At 24 a shut eye was the open hole turned on its side,
# 4 cells wide by 2 tall, because a 1-row slit at 3x is a rendering seam rather than an eyelid. Here
# each is a curve: a 17 x 8 lash of 89 cells bowing down over its own socket, drawn from the same
# arithmetic that places the eyes so a lid cannot drift off the socket it is closing.
#
# The z's are 9x9 glyphs with 3-thick bars, 63 cells each, and they step three columns left and eleven
# rows up as they rise: columns 62-70 on rows 22-30, then 59-67 on rows 11-19, then 56-64 on rows 0-8.
# Both gaps are load-bearing. Eleven rows of pitch against a 9-tall glyph leaves two clear rows
# between them; ten leaves one, and nine has them touching and merged into a bar. The lowest z is held
# four columns clear of the dome or it reads as a horn growing out of the creature rather than as a
# breath leaving it.
#
# Four frames: 1 z, 2 z, 3 z, then the lowest one gone. The stream fills, then the bottom of it
# dissipates while a new one starts below, so the loop restarts without every z popping at once. At
# SLEEP_FRAME_MS that is a 6 s cycle -- slow enough that the count is readable as a count.
#
# The body breathes underneath on the even/odd frames: 2238 cells of silhouette neutral against 2207
# settled, both within twenty of the base grid's 2224, the settle three rows shorter and four columns
# wider. The z's are ink on top of that and not part of the creature, which is why the frame totals
# climb 2054, 2086, 2180, 2086 while the slime itself does not change size.
#
# The sleeping pose is flatter and wider than the idle: awake the slime holds a dome, asleep it
# relaxes toward a puddle and the floor is where the volume goes. Every frame rests on row 67 --
# nothing about sleep leaves the ground.

# Fight stance for slime: two frames, hand-authored, because the derived fight is the dome shoved

# direction at all. `fight` is the one state that must face its target: ACTION_FIGHT_LEFT is this grid
# mirrored and it is what the enemy plays.
#
# The turn is carried by the whole mass, because a blob has nothing else to turn with. There is no far
# ear to occlude and no limb to put edge-on, and moving the eyes inside a symmetric dome reads as a
# lazy eye rather than as a head turn. So the profile itself goes asymmetric: the rear falls away from
# the crest in one long slope while the leading edge stands up steep and boxy -- the same superellipse

#

# animation's: a hammer at 2224 cells is a bar joined to a bar of its own width, which is a bent tube
# rather than a T, and a wave at 2224 is a curl with no sea under it, which is a shell. What each one

# them. All three face right; the mirrors are generated and the enemy plays those.
#
# Rock: the slime does not pick a hammer up, it becomes one. There is no torso in either frame -- the
# whole silhouette is head and handle, and the eyes punched into the head are the only thing left

# on a stick, because the body ate the columns the head needed to be wide in.
#

# handle drops from under its middle down to the left. Frame 1 is the impact, a rotation of about
# ninety degrees rather than a drop -- the butt stays down at the left and the head swings from the top
# of the canvas to a slab standing on rows 18-67 at columns 36-68. That the slab reaches the floor row

# first and the result was an elbow -- one continuous stroke bending, because a bar with the shaft

# Paper: the slime does not throw water, it becomes the wave.
#
# The curl is a hook and not a ring, and that took two tries. An annulus with a quarter bitten out of
# it reads as a doughnut however the bite is aimed, because the barrel then encloses a hole and a hole
# ringed by ink has no direction in it. Drawn as a hook -- a stroke rising out of the water on the
# left, arcing right across the top and falling -- the barrel is open to the background on the right,
# which is the only way its mouth stays a mouth and stays pointed at the target.
#
# The lip is thrown out past the mouth to column 56 and then tucks back inward under itself, tapering
# from 11 cells to 5 as it goes, and it stops well short of the water. A hook that only descends is a
# wall of water; one that closes on the sea below is the doughnut again. The inward tuck between those
# two is the whole difference between a breaking wave and a slope.
#
# The band of water it stands on runs rows 46-67 across nearly the full width, with its surface
# stepped in over four rows rather than cut square -- at this size a square corner reads as a crate the
# wave is standing on. The band is load-bearing in the same way the hook is: a curl drawn on its own
# is a shell, and the flat water under it is what makes it read as sea.
#
# The face stays in the water and out from under the curl -- 8x11 sockets at columns 38-45 and 52-59
# over rows 54-64, with the gloss off to the left at columns 10-24. It cannot ride the arc: the hook
# is eleven cells thick at its thinnest and an eye punched through it eats the stroke the wave is made
# of.
#
# Frame 1 is the pitch. The whole curl rolls two columns right and two rows down and the lip extends
# further out and further under, so the barrel narrows from above while its mouth stays open. The

# Scissors: a chakram, a ring of goo carried high and forward on frame 0 and thrown down and further
# forward on frame 1 -- the ring travels three columns right and five rows down between them.
#
# It is carried out past the flank rather than over the crest, and that is the second attempt. Held
# above the middle of the dome the hoop read as a head and the arm under it as a neck, which turns a
# blob into a figure -- and the one thing every pose of this creature has to keep saying is that it has
# no neck to have. Out to the side, overlapping the flank, the same hoop reads as held.
#
# The ring stays attached to the body by an arm tapering out of the shoulder, and that is not
# decoration either. A hoop floating clear of the creature is a second object, and this slime does not
# pick weapons up -- every attack it has is its own body reshaped. The arm is what says the ring is
# still part of it, and it is drawn as a stalk tapering 10 cells to 6 rather than as a column, for the
# same reason the ring moved off the crest.
#
# The ring is 31 across with an 8-thick wall, leaving a hole of 141 cells and thirteen across -- big
# enough to still be a hole after the mirror. Any thinner and the wall breaks up at 1x; any smaller and
# the middle closes and the chakram becomes a ball.
#
# The body under it is the creature, squatter than the idle and with the same face: sockets over rows
# 49-60, gloss found a place on the left flank because the arm takes the crest it normally sits on.
# 2169 and 2125 cells: the pitch runs forty-four lighter, which is the ring sinking into the flank as
# it comes down, and nothing else changing size.
